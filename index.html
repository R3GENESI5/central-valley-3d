About this page

What the colours rest on. Valley floor: one USGS CVHM2 land-use class per 1-mile model cell for each year from 1874 to 2019, grouped as in the project's era plates (water, groundwater-fed native, dry native, rice, orchard and vine, field crops, pasture, idle, irrigated unspecified, urban). Where a cell is native, the colour follows the pre-1900 historic vegetation map (Central Valley Historic Mapping Project, Geographical Information Center, CSU Chico; obtained from Data Basin, Conservation Biology Institute) at 300 m: tule marsh, riparian forest, valley oak, grassland, alkali scrub, floodplain. Before 1874 every floor cell is shown as the pre-1900 map. After 2019 the floor stays at 2019. Hills and the land outside the CVHM2 cells: USGS Annual NLCD 2025 at 300 m.

What is estimated. A native cell that the pre-1900 map shows as marsh, lake, riparian or floodplain, but that CVHM2 no longer calls groundwater-fed, is drawn as drained native land (dry grass). NLCD 2025 is projected back in time for the hills: reservoirs appear only from the year their dam was built (GDW reservoir outlines; Lake Tahoe, a natural lake held by a dam, stays water); towns and farms outside the floor are shown as grassland or oak savanna before rough start years (dense urban 1900, medium 1940, low 1960, open developed 1980, farmland 1880). Mountains and hills change with three dated records: forest stand age (Pan et al. 2011, 1 km, about 2006) gives the year each stand last established, so the stand is drawn as older forest before that year, cleared for five years, then regrowing; Hansen forest loss (2001 to 2023) draws forest before the loss year, a burnt or cut scar for four years, then the 2025 cover returning; and Annual NLCD gives the first year (1990 to 2025) each town pixel was developed. Stand age is read as time since the last stand-replacing disturbance (logging or fire), which is an estimate. Fires come from the CAL FIRE perimeters (20,802 fires, 1878 to 2025, simplified to about 50 m): a burnt pixel shows a scar for the fire year and the next two (one year on grass), then regrowth over twelve years; up to the four most recent fires per pixel are kept. Perimeter records before about 1950 are incomplete. Field patterns, season colours and snow (above about 1,800 m in winter) are drawn to look like the land, not measured. Terrain: Copernicus GLO-90, drawn 4 times higher than real.

Kinds of layer. Each layer says what it is: measured, mapped, estimated or model. Numbers on the page are copied from the project's test records, which each card and chapter names.

California's Central Valley, before 1900 to 2025

The case: the valley floor lost its water: drained, dammed and pumped. Since then the low air over it warms and dries faster after rain, no longer builds its own fog, and turns less of the same ocean vapour into rain.

The other explanation: a change in the weather from the Pacific (less vapour, a warmer sea, more stable air aloft) would show at sea, aloft and on the mountain slope as much as on the floor. The walkthrough shows both, place by place.
